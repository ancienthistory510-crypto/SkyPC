      Beginner's Guide to PC Building

      Everything you need to assemble your first PC with confidence.

      1. Plan Before You Buy

      Decide your primary use — gaming, work, or both — then set a budget. Allocate roughly: GPU ~35%, CPU ~25%, and spread the rest across motherboard, RAM, storage, PSU, case, and cooling.

      2. Check Compatibility First

      The CPU and motherboard must share the same socket, and RAM must match the motherboard's memory type (DDR4 vs DDR5). The case must support the motherboard's form factor. Use the compatibility panel on the Builder tab — it checks all of this automatically.

      3. Don't Skimp on the PSU

      Your power supply powers everything. Buy one with at least 100–150W of headroom over your estimated load, and prefer reputable brands with 80 Plus Bronze certification or better. A good PSU protects all your other parts.

      4. Physical Fit Matters

      Graphics cards are long, coolers are tall, and cases have limits. Check GPU clearance, cooler height, and radiator support in your case specs before ordering.

      5. Assembly Order

      Install the CPU, RAM, and M.2 SSD onto the motherboard before mounting it in the case. Then add the cooler, GPU, storage drives, and finally the PSU. Cable management comes last — route cables behind the motherboard tray.

      6. First Boot

      Connect monitor, keyboard, and power. Enter BIOS (usually Del or F2 at startup) to confirm everything is detected. Then install your OS from a USB drive. If fans spin but there's no display, reseat the RAM and GPU first.

      7. Drivers & Updates

      Install chipset drivers from your motherboard manufacturer's site, GPU drivers from NVIDIA/AMD/Intel, and update your BIOS if a newer version improves stability or compatibility.

      8. Keep It Cool & Clean

      Ensure case fans move air front-to-back. Dust builds up — clean filters every few months. Monitoring temps with free tools like HWMonitor helps you catch problems early.
